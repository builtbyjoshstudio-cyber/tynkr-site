01

The cloud is just someone else's computer. Keep yours.

Why local-first

Every Tynkr app stores your work in files on your machine instead of a cloud database. Keep, move and back up your folders however you like — or don't. When we shut down someday, your software keeps working.

No servers

We never see your data

No telemetry, no analytics, no accounts — none of the apps send us your data. Ledger, Vault, Hearth and Forge make no network calls of their own; Inkpress only loads remote images during print PDF export if your manuscript links to the web (and uses WebView2 / Edge or Chrome). Microsoft handles Store purchases, licenses and updates.

Open formats

Files you can actually read

Your ledger is JSON. Your notes are Markdown. Your formatted books are EPUB, PDF, HTML and .inkpress.json. No proprietary database, no export hostage-taking — and in Ledger, Vault, Hearth and Forge, optional password protection encrypts your files.

No rent

Buy once. Own forever.

One-time purchase per app. No monthly toll, no account to create, no license keys to enter and nothing phoning home to us. Software the way it used to work.

Suite questions.

FAQ
How does pricing work across the Tynkr desktop apps?

Every Tynkr app uses a pay-once model — a one-time purchase per app through the Microsoft Store when it launches. Bug fixes are always free, major version upgrades are a separate purchase, and there are no subscriptions, no accounts, and no in-app upgrades.

Where will the Tynkr apps be available, and when do they launch?

All five Tynkr apps are in development and coming soon for Windows. They will be certified, signed, sold, and delivered only through the Microsoft Store, with updates delivered automatically through the Store. You can join the Tynkr Tools & Co Substack list below for launch notifications.

Do the Tynkr apps work offline or send data over the internet?

None of the five apps have accounts, telemetry, or analytics, and none send your data to Built by Josh Studio LLC. Tynkr Ledger, Vault, Hearth, and Forge make no network calls of their own and work completely offline. Tynkr Inkpress makes one exception: during print PDF export, it downloads remote images linked from the web in your manuscript (use local images on your PC to avoid any connection); Inkpress also uses Microsoft Edge WebView2 (which includes SmartScreen) and Microsoft Edge or Google Chrome for print PDF export.

Where are my files stored, and does Tynkr encrypt them?

Every Tynkr app stores your work in ordinary files on your own Windows PC — JSON, Markdown, EPUB, PDF, and HTML — and uninstalling an app leaves your data in place. Tynkr Ledger, Vault, Hearth, and Forge save in a folder you choose and offer optional password protection that encrypts your files on disk. Tynkr Inkpress saves preferences and default exports in Documents\Tynkr Inkpress plus a .inkpress.json sidecar next to each manuscript without encrypting them; those files are protected by your Windows account.
